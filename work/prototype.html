A productive fall trip starts with knowing when to change things up. Luke’s four-lure rotation moves from a surface presentation to a searching plug, then to softer, quieter options as the bite changes.

01 Moonwalker02 Skinny Lipper XL03 Mulligan Bomber04 Prawn Original
01

Start with the surface bite.

MOONWALKER · TOPWATER

When the fish are feeding aggressively, especially early in the day, Luke reaches for the 4-inch Moonwalker in a mullet pattern. Its single inline hooks help keep smaller fish from interrupting a shot at a bigger trout.

Your cue to switch: Surface strikes taper off? Keep covering water with the Skinny Lipper XL.
02

Find the fish on big flats.

SKINNY LIPPER XL · SEARCHING PLUG

The Skinny Lipper XL makes long casts across open flats. Work it steadily, give it a slashbait retrieve, or let it rise after diving. It’s Luke’s next move when the topwater bite fades.

03

Go softer. Keep the big profile.

MULLIGAN BOMBER · 5-INCH PADDLETAIL

The Mulligan Bomber brings a large baitfish profile with a softer presentation. Luke changes its rigging with the conditions: a Hoss Helix weighted hook around shallow cover, or a jighead to reach deeper water.

Rig it for your water. to compare the two setups.
04

Get subtle with spooky fish.

PRAWN ORIGINAL · 4.3-INCH SHRIMP

For wary fish, Luke turns to the Prawn Original’s quieter shrimp presentation. Like the Bomber, it works on a weighted hook in shallow water or a jighead when more depth is needed.
